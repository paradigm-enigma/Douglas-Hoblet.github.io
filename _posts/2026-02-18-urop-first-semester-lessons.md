I started my UROP at CSAIL in September with enthusiasm, no idea what I was doing,
and the vague suspicion that my mentor thought I knew more than I did.
(She didn't, but only because she's been doing this long enough to pattern-match
nervous sophomores.) Here's what sophomore-me would have paid to hear on day one.

## 1. The first month is about vocabulary, not results

Your first month is mostly reading. You'll feel unproductive.
That's the job. A paper a day + a weekly 1:1 where you explain
what confused you is worth more than code you can't justify.

## 2. Your mentor is not your professor

They have their own deadlines, their own dissertation, and their own advisor
yelling at them. Send a weekly status email even if they didn't ask for one.
It makes their life easier and makes you memorable in a good way.

## 3. Write down everything

Not in Slack. Not in your head. In a real lab notebook — a Markdown file
per week is plenty. Future you will thank present you when you're writing
up results in April and can't remember which hyperparameter you changed in October.

## 4. The [UROP funding deadlines](https://urop.mit.edu/funding) are real

Direct funding applications are due mid-semester for the *following* term.
Miss it and you're either doing for-credit (fine) or for-volunteer (less fine).
I put all four deadlines in my calendar the day I signed on.

## 5. Publishable > impressive

A small, clean, reproducible result is worth ten half-finished clever ideas.
The `6.8610` final projects I respected most were the ones that picked a boring
question and answered it honestly.
